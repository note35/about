Ever wonder how a panel discussion goes from a vague idea to a main-stage event? Let me pull back the curtain on my experience moderating at PyCon TW 2025—a journey that took eight months.

Panel discussions are deceptively difficult. Done well, they're the highlight of a conference. Done poorly, they're a cringeworthy waste of everyone's time. This might be why PyCon TW, a more than 10 years old conference, had only hosted [one Chinese-language panel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HDwnfhYl5Fg) in 2023.

So, what makes them so tough? In this article, I'm sharing the full playbook: the strategy, the unexpected hurdles, and the hard-won lessons from my 8 months-long adventure in panel moderation.


# The Idea That Wouldn't Let Go

My push for a panel at PyCon TW actually started two years ago. In 2023, inspired by the incredible virtual panels at PyCon APAC 2021, I championed the idea within the Program team. I kept asking, "Why don't we have this at PyCon TW?"

But when the opportunity arose to moderate that first panel myself, I declined. Imposter syndrome hit hard. The thought of leading our first-ever panel on a topic like LLM, where I wasn't an expert, was daunting. We were fortunate that YYC, our conference founder, stepped in and masterfully handled that milestone session.

Still, the idea never left me. Attending PyCon US 2023 and EuroPython 2024, I saw again and again how central panel discussions were to the community. Having given eight talks at PyCon TW since 2017, I knew I was ready for a new challenge. It was time to stop just advocating for a panel and finally step up to the moderator's podium.


# Defining the Roadmap

The decision was made on a snowy slope. While shredding the backcountry with Justin, the PyCon TW 2025 Programme Lead, I impulsively signed up to be the event’s moderator. Instantly my mind started to map out the mountain of work that lay ahead. It all boiled down to a handful of core questions that would steer me for the next months.

- **What does a moderator actually do?** More than just firing off questions, a moderator shapes the conversation, keeps the pace, and pulls the audience into the dialogue.
- **What topic can sustain an hour of genuine interest?** The subject had to be compelling enough to hold the crowd’s attention from start to finish.
- **Which voices will bring that topic to life?** Selecting the right panelists was only the first step; I also had to design a pre‑discussion plan so that the on‑stage conversation would feel cohesive, not like a series of disconnected Q&A snippets.
- **How do I orchestrate the logistics?** From staging an experimental recording session during the pre‑conference speaker night to coordinating the final schedule and stage layout, every detail had to fit together like a well‑tuned instrument.

With those questions in hand, the road from idea to execution became a clear, if challenging, path.

## 🧭 Step 0 – The Moderator’s Playbook

It’s funny—I’ve attended dozens of panel discussions, but I realized I’d never truly watched the moderator. My attention, like everyone else’s, was always on the panelists and their answers. I quickly learned that a great moderator is like a great film editor: their work is invisible, but they shape the entire experience.

The role is far more than just opening remarks and a closing thank you. I broke down the moderator's core responsibilities into four key areas:

- **Architect the Conversation**: Don't just ask questions; structure them into themed sections. My job was to guide the discussion from one point to the next with smooth transitions and insightful follow-up questions.
- **Manage the Clock**: This is non-negotiable. I did some "panel math" and realized that in a 60-minute session, you only have time for about five major questions. A complex question involving all panelists can easily take 7-10 minutes, while a quick one for a single person takes 3-5.
- **Balance the Voices**: Ensure every panelist gets a chance to shine and that questions are distributed evenly. No one should dominate the conversation, and no one should be left out.
- **Be the Host**: Ultimately, the moderator's job is to create a welcoming and energetic atmosphere on stage, making both the panelists and the audience feel comfortable.

To learn how to put this into practice, I created my own study guide by analyzing a few master moderators.


### The Study Guide: Inside the Minds of Seasoned Moderators

- **The Masterclass in Facilitation: [Adam Smith](https://www.nobelprize.org/events/nobel-prize-dialogue/tokyo-2025/panellists/adam-smith-2-3-2-3/) ([Nobel Prize Dialogue Tokyo 2025](https://www.nobelprize.org/the-impact-of-ai/))**: Having attended this event for years, I knew Adam was the one to watch. His panels feature Nobel laureates—literally the smartest people in their fields. **My Takeaway**: He never pretends to be an expert. Instead, he excels at making complex topics accessible and guiding brilliant minds through a coherent conversation. This was the style I wanted to emulate.
- **The Expert-Led Deep Dive: [The CPython Panel (EuroPython 2025)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0j8euKVjirg)**: This panel is moderated by CPython Core Developers Pablo and Łukasz. It’s a great example of what to do when the moderator is also a domain expert. **My Takeaway**: They use their deep knowledge to ask sharp, insightful follow-ups. This taught me how to leverage my own expertise on certain topics without dominating the discussion.
- **The Blueprint for Structure: [The Closing Panel (EuroPython 2025)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sf2AqQ5a38Y)**: Here, the conference organizers moderated a diverse group of prominent community members. **My Takeaway**: Their rundown was crystal clear. They demonstrated how to create a solid narrative arc that carries the discussion from beginning to end, which became the blueprint for my own panel's structure.


## 🎯 Step 1 – Sparking Curiosity: Picking a Topic That Keeps an Audience Hooked

The initial spark came from a conversation with Justin. We were both fascinated by AI's real-world impact on software engineers. Our curiosity sharpened about eight months before the conference when headlines were reporting [an all-time low in engineering job postings](https://www.reddit.com/r/hackernews/comments/1iuk0vp/software_engineering_job_openings_hit_fiveyear_low/). The question wasn't just academic anymore; it felt urgent and deeply relevant to our community.

At first, I settled on a broad theme: "The Impact of AI." It felt like a smart, strategic choice. The topic was hot, guaranteeing audience interest, and its breadth made it easy to find panelists. I figured I could invite experts from various fields and simply tailor questions to their backgrounds.
But a key observation at EuroPython made me rethink everything. I noticed that in public forums, speakers were reluctant to address the core fear directly: "Will AI replace humans?" The conversation always seemed to dance around the issue.

That's when it clicked. A generic "AI Impact" discussion wasn't enough. We needed to tackle the underlying tension head-on, but in a way that was constructive, not just fear-mongering. I reframed the entire panel around a sharper, more provocative question: **"Coexisting with AI: Will Experts Adapt or Lead?"**.

This new title did three things perfectly: it acknowledged the challenge ("Coexisting"), presented the two possible paths ("Adapt or Lead"), and transformed a vague topic into a focused, compelling debate.


## 🔗 Step 2 – Building Your Dream Panel: Selecting Voices That Amplify the Conversation

With a compelling topic locked in, the next challenge was finding the right minds to discuss it. From the start, I had a few non-negotiable principles for building the panel:

1. **Diversity is Essential**: I needed a mix of genders, industries, and backgrounds to ensure a rich conversation, not an echo chamber.
2. **Local Representation Matters**: Since our keynotes are often international, I knew we needed a strong voice to represent the vibrant tech community here in Taiwan.
3. **Be Realistic**: Our primary candidates would be our keynote speakers. My backup plan was to tap into the pool of talented speakers whose proposals were accepted through our CFP.

This whole phase was a calculated risk. We were sending out keynote invitations without knowing who would accept, let alone agree to the extra commitment of a panel. To mitigate this, I built a shadow list of potential candidates I could contact if things went south. We sent the invitations, crossed our fingers, and waited.

Then, the good news started rolling in. To my immense relief, every confirmed keynote speaker accepted my invitation to join the panel!
We had an incredible lineup with a fantastic mix of industry experience and gender diversity. But when I looked at the roster, I spotted the gap I’d been worried about: including myself, everyone was based internationally.

This is where Justin had a brilliant idea. He suggested inviting YC from MediaTek. As a local expert in the semi-conductor industry who had published AI-related research, he was the perfect final piece of the puzzle.


## 🧠 Step 3 – Immersive Preparation: The AI Deep Dive

Our team was assembled. Now, it was time to figure out what we were going to talk about.

### Part1: From Novice to Navigator

A moderator is supposed to be the expert on stage, right?

Eight months before the conference, I wasn't. [My speaking background](https://note35.github.io/about/talks) was in data engineering with Apache Beam; I never imagined I’d be the one leading a conversation on AI. To guide this panel authentically, I knew my first and most important task was to educate myself. I had to become the moderator this topic deserved.

I created a total immersion plan that covered theory, community sentiment, and practical application.

#### Building the Foundation (The Theory)

- I completed my company's entire AI basics curriculum, covering Transformers, Reinforcement Learning, Fine-tuning, and Prompt Engineering.
- I went back and finished some AI math classes I had previously put on hold.

#### Constant Immersion (The Community)

- Listening & Watching: I consumed 1-2 podcasts and 3-5 YouTube videos on AI advancements every single week.
- Field Research: At EuroPython 2025, my schedule was packed with AI-related talks.
- Human Barometer: I constantly asked people I met—from students to retirees—the same questions: "Do you use AI? How? Are you worried it will replace your job?" (If a random Asian guy in Europe asked you this, it was probably me.)

#### Practical Application (The Tools)

- I integrated AI coding assistants like Cursor and Vibe Code into my daily development workflow.
- I used AI as a learning partner for everything, from suggesting new swimming techniques to generating recipes for dinner.

The result of this deep dive was transformative. I didn't just gain knowledge; I gained a nuanced perspective. My initial pessimism about AI replacing humans evolved into concrete, optimistic advice on how we can coexist with it. I could finally explain AGI definition, detail the limitations of transformers, and, most importantly, I felt the confidence I needed to moderate.

For anyone grappling with AI anxiety, my advice is simple: dive in. Active, hands-on learning is the best antidote to fear.

### Part 2: Crafting the Conversation

My research wasn't just for my own confidence; it was the foundation for the panel's agenda. I followed one golden rule: **Ask questions your panelists are uniquely qualified to answer.**

We finalized the agenda about two weeks before the conference, shortly after OpenAI's GPT-5 launch, and sent it to the panelists. Since our panel included a CPython Core Developer, an Open Source expert, a Sports Scientist, and an industry researcher (YC), the questions had to be precisely tailored.

This meant strategically skipping generic, philosophical debates. For instance, I cut the question, "Is an LLM just searching or is it truly thinking?" because we didn't have a foundational model researcher on stage. Asking it would have led to speculation, not insight.

Instead, the questions were laser-focused on their domains:

- How is AI impacting the job market for developers?
- What are the practical AI breakthroughs in academic research?
- How can open-source projects leverage "vibe coding" with AI?
- Where is AI driving real product innovation?

This targeted approach is the key to a valuable panel. It respects the experts' time and ensures the audience leaves with credible, insightful answers.


## 🌟 Step 4 (bonus)- The Speaker Night Experiment: Testing Chemistry Before the Big Day

My goal was bigger than just one panel. I wanted the "Coexisting with AI" theme to permeate the entire conference. Inspired by the [Teaching Python Podcast's open-space](https://www.teachingpython.fm/148) format, I pitched a wild idea to Justin: what if we turned a corner of our Speaker Night dinner into a pop-up recording studio?

He loved it. With a team of incredible volunteers, we set up a recording table right in the middle of a bustling buffet restaurant. Over the course of the evening, we interviewed 25 speakers—from first-timers to our keynotes—asking them the same candid questions: "How do you really use AI? And are you worried about being replaced?"

The magic didn't stop there. In a heroic, all-night effort, our volunteers edited the raw footage into a 30-minute highlight reel. We played it in every conference room during lunchtime the very next day, just hours before the panel. It was our way of starting the conversation before the panel even began, creating a shared context for the entire audience.

But this experiment was also a data-gathering mission. To make sense of hours of interviews, I turned to AI itself, feeding the transcripts into NotebookLM to distill the key themes. The results were fascinating and confirmed my own journey:

- A clear majority (roughly 19 of 25) were not afraid of being replaced.
- They viewed AI as a powerful productivity booster.
- Their anxiety was inversely proportional to their usage—the more they used AI, the less they feared it.

This data was my final confidence boost. It proved that our panel's proactive, optimistic message was exactly what the community was feeling.

This whole process also revealed how breathtakingly fast the landscape is changing. In early 2025, "using AI" meant drafting emails. Today, engineers are deep into "vibe coding," treating AI as a genuine collaborator. It’s becoming our unlimited intern, which is precisely why we need events like PyCon to come together and figure out what it all means.


## 🚀 Step 5 – Orchestrating the Event: Final Logistics & Surprise Moments

With the content and panelists locked in, my focus shifted to the final logistics. It’s easy to dismiss these as "minor details," but the when, where, and how of a panel can completely change its energy. Believe me, nothing was trivial.

### The AI‑Themed Schedule

Our first decision was timing. The programme team strategically placed the panel on the afternoon of the first day. This wasn't just about keynote availability; it was about creating a narrative. Combined with the speaker night video at lunch and an AI-focused morning keynote, we effectively built an "AI Theme Day," immersing attendees in the topic from multiple angles before our discussion even began.

### Seating Dynamics for Interactive Dialogue

While our amazing chairperson and venue team handled the physical setup of the sofas and stage, I obsessed over the seating chart. My goal was to be a conversation chemist. Instead of grouping people by background, I intentionally separated our two open-source experts and our two academically experienced panelists. I wanted to create a dynamic where they would engage across disciplines, hoping to spark some lively "chemical effects" and unexpected debates.

### Embracing Happy Accidents

Of course, you can't plan for everything. We walked on stage to a last-minute surprise: only two microphones for five people. What could have been a disaster turned into a blessing in disguise. One of our wonderful keynote speakers volunteered to act as the official mic-passer. This small, spontaneous act of teamwork instantly broke the ice, creating a warm, collaborative atmosphere that no amount of planning could have ever achieved. It was a perfect reminder that sometimes, the unscripted moments are the best.


The impact of those last‑minute choices was measurable.

- 10 + minutes of live Q&A – the longest interactive segment we’d run for this format.
- The panel finished 1 minute over schedule while still leaving a queue of unanswered questions.
- The sheer number of on‑spot queries proved the audience was genuinely invested in the topic, turning what began as a logistical hiccup into the talk’s most energising element.

These concrete results not only validated the “AI Theme Day” narrative but also underscored that a single mic‑passer can become the catalyst for an engaging, on‑stage chemistry.


# Conclusion: Was It Worth It?

So, after eight months of planning, research, and wrestling with my own AI anxiety—was it all worth it?

The answer is a resounding yes. This journey did more than just teach me how to moderate a panel. After nine years of giving talks, I’d forgotten the thrill of a new challenge. This experience rekindled a passion for contributing in a different way. It wasn't a perfect performance, and I have so much more to learn, but it left me hungry to step onto that stage again and do it even better.

More importantly, it solidified my perspective on why we all gather for conferences in the first place. I started this process with the same fear about AI that many in our industry share, and I felt a deep responsibility to help guide that conversation toward a more optimistic place.

It brings me back to a point I made in a lightning talk in the Python Tokyo meetup earlier this year: in an age where AI can answer almost any question, the value of human connection becomes paramount. AI can give us information, but it can’t replicate the spark of a live debate or the energy of a community coming together to navigate the future.
