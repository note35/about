This year marked a special shift for me. At past PyCons, I've always prioritized sessions about the Python language itself. However, given the current excitement around LLMs, I decided to fully immerse myself in artificial intelligence, approaching EuroPython as if it were an AI-focused event.

# About EuroPython 2025

EuroPython is the longest-running Python conference, established in 2002. The 2025 conference attracted approximately 1,500 participants and spanned seven days. This included two days of tutorials and summits, three days of main conference talks, and two days dedicated to development sprints. Notably, roughly 26% of all sessions were directly classified under AI, Machine Learning, and Data, highlighting a significant focus on these areas.

# AI-related Sessions Takeaways

## Tutorial: Accelerating Scientific Python: JAX, Numba, and Ray in Action

This tutorial demonstrated the implementation and performance comparison of JAX and Numba for scientific computing tasks, exemplified through a k-nearest neighbor search. It further explored the integration of these high-performance libraries with Ray for distributed execution. The session provided practical guidance on optimizing performance within specific contexts, rather than offering a definitive comparative framework.

Key Insights: 1) **AI for Productivity**: The use of Gemini within Colab environments proved highly beneficial in rapidly resolving complex programming challenges during exercises, highlighting their potential to significantly enhance developer productivity. Almost zero questions are asked, and many audiences help each other by sharing their prompt result. 2) **Engineering Effort for Optimization**: Despite the availability of powerful acceleration tools, selecting the optimal solution (JAX and Numba) for a given problem is non-trivial and requires considerable engineering effort to understand the specific performance characteristics and trade-offs of each.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/accelerating-scientific-python-jax-numba-and-ray-in-action), [Github](https://github.com/coobas/europython-25)

## Talk: Accelerating privacy-enhancing data processing

This session showcased an infrastructure designed for privacy-compliant processing of highly sensitive data, such as patient records in hospital environments. The presented architecture bridges scientists, engineers, and clinicians, utilizing tools like Jupyter (Colab), Dagster (a workflow orchestrator similar to Apache Airflow), and data integration solutions such as dltHub, dbt, and DuckDB, often integrating with BigQuery equivalents.

**Key Insight**: The architectural patterns and tooling discussed provide valuable parallels to my employer's internal strategies for managing and processing sensitive data.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/accelerating-privacy-enhancing-data-processing)

## Talk: Fairlearn:practical approach to assessing and mitigating harm in AI systems 

This talk introduced [fairlearn.org](http://fairlearn.org), an open-source toolkit designed to assess and mitigate biases and harms in AI systems. The presentation provided practical examples and references, illustrating various bias problems and methods for addressing them effectively within AI development workflows.

**Key Insight**: While my employer maintains internal initiatives like [Fairness Indicators for scalable fair ML systems](https://research.google/blog/fairness-indicators-scalable-infrastructure-for-fair-ml-systems/), the Fairlearn toolkit offers a complementary external perspective on practical approaches to AI fairness. Evaluating such open-source tools can inform our understanding of community best practices and potential strategies for enhancing our internal fairness assessment and mitigation efforts.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/fairlearn-practical-approach-to-assessing-and-mitigating-harm-in-ai-systems), [Slide](https://pitch.com/v/short-fairlearn-intro-u25bfc)

## Talk: Is Prompt Engineering Dead? How Auto-Optimization is Changing the Game

This session explored the shift from manual prompt engineering to automatic prompt optimization frameworks. The speakers highlighted how tools like DSPy, TextGrad, and AdalFlow are leveraging training data and advanced techniques to create self-improving LLM pipelines, thereby streamlining the development of robust LLM applications.

**Key Insight**: This discussion provided valuable external perspectives on the evolving landscape of LLM development, resonating with many company's agent-focused efforts. Understanding the challenges and tooling adopted by the broader AI community offers critical insights into problem-solving approaches and effective frameworks for our own LLM initiatives.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/is-prompt-engineering-dead-how-auto-optimization-is-changing-the-game/), [Slide]()

## Talk: EU AI Act: Unveiling Lesser-Known Aspects and Implementation Entities

This session provided a detailed overview of the EU AI Act, covering its foundational principles, categories of banned and high-risk AI systems, and key exceptions. It also shed light on the entities responsible for its implementation, offering a clearer understanding of the regulatory framework.

**Key Insight**: This legislative knowledge is highly valuable for engineers working on features for the EU market. While large corp has Legal Counsel generally responsible for final compliance, an informed engineering team can proactively design features that align with the Act's requirements from the outset. This forward-thinking approach is critical for mitigating compliance risks and preventing potential delays in the deployment of AI-powered products.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/eu-ai-act-unveiling-lesser-known-aspects-and-implementation-entities), [EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/)

## Talk: The Pydantic Stack for AI Apps

The speaker live-demonstrated the development of an AI application (a blog post writer) using the Pydantic ecosystem. This talk highlighted Pydantic's solutions for iteratively structuring and validating LLM outputs, a critical aspect of building reliable AI applications.

**Key Insight**: Pydantic is emerging as one of the industry leaders for building AI applications due to its effective methods for debugging and ensuring the reliability of LLM outputs. Its focus on structured data validation, coupled with tools for performance monitoring, provides a powerful and proactive approach to preventing errors and streamlining the development lifecycle for AI-powered products.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/the-pydantic-stack-for-ai-apps), [Guide](https://ai.pydantic.dev/)

## Talk: Meet Marimo, the Next-Gen Notebook

This talk introduced Marimo, a next-generation notebook environment designed to address the inherent challenges of traditional notebooks (jupyter, colab), such as hidden state and reproducibility issues. Marimo achieves this through a reactive execution model that guarantees a consistent program state. The platform also enhances notebooks by making them more interactive with built-in UI elements, easier to maintain as pure Python files, and more versatile for sharing as web apps or reusable modules.

**Key Insight**: Marimo's vision represents a significant leap in notebook technology, particularly given the involvement of Google-affiliated investors and a former Google Brain founder. While the timeline for a similar transformation within Colab is not yet clear, closely monitoring Marimo's development and its reactive execution model is crucial for staying ahead of the curve in reproducible and interactive data science tooling.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/meet-marimo-the-next-gen-notebook), [Github](https://github.com/marimo-team/marimo)

## Talk: Wisdom of the Cloud? Drifting through Community Notes

This talk explored X's Community Notes, a system designed to address misinformation and other real-world challenges on social media. Through numerous examples, the speaker illustrated how the solution, while effective, also introduced new avenues for abuse. This underscored the continued critical need for human-in-the-loop oversight, even in the AI-hype world.

**Key Insight**: The Community Notes project serves as a valuable case study for our own product development, particularly when considering LLM-based solutions. It highlights the complex trade-offs between automated systems and human involvement, offering crucial lessons on how to build robust, abuse-resistant products that leverage AI effectively while maintaining quality and ethical standards.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/wisdom-of-the-cloud-drifting-through-community-notes), [Github](https://github.com/twitter/communitynotes)

## Talk by me: Beyond the Code: Manipulating Bytecode and Building Community

During this presentation, I shared insights on Pytype, a bytecode-based type checker widely used in AI-related Python projects in Google. The talk highlighted the community consensus that the engineering cost of maintaining such a tool is increasingly unsustainable, especially when compared to AST-based alternatives. This sentiment is driven by the consistent rise in type annotation adoption, which has diminished the need for tools focused on untyped code coverage.

**Key Insight**: The community's perspective on the high maintenance cost and diminishing utility of bytecode-based type checkers directly validates Google's strategic direction on Python type checking.

Reference: [Talk](https://ep2025.europython.eu/session/beyond-the-code-manipulating-bytecode-and-building-community), [Slide](https://github.com/note35/about/blob/dev/public/slides/2025-bytecode.pdf)

# Discussions Takeaways

## Panel Discussion

The panel discussion provided several key insights for navigating the future of software development with AI:
- **Automation**: The panel strongly advocated for increasing the use of auto-generated pull requests to enhance efficiency and streamline workflows.
- **Adaptability**: It is becoming crucial for engineers and leaders to adapt to non-traditional communication, such as addressing unconventional questions from "vibe coders."
- **Human-Centric Roles**: The majority of panelists agreed that critical roles requiring human input and creative problem-solving are secure, with little concern for wholesale replacement by AI.
- **Professional Development**: The importance of continuous learning was emphasized, highlighting the need for individuals to gain and maintain expertise relevant to their specific positions.
- **Ethical Mindset**: A strong ethical mindset is essential when developing and deploying AI solutions to ensure responsible and beneficial outcomes.

## Private Discussion

I had private discussions with many attendees revealed several key insights regarding the current and future impact of AI, particularly LLMs, on the tech industry:

- **AI Adoption**: 100% of people have tried using AI in their work and are open to its adoption.
Perceived Limitations of AI: Over 80% of participants believe that AI is not yet "smart enough" and will not surpass their own intelligence in their lifetime. They pointed to several reasons why LLMs are not yet suitable for their work, including:
- **Closed Work Environments**: Restrictions on internet access and outdated hardware.
Proprietary Data: Their business models are based on highly private data.
- **Immature Solutions**: The lack of mature open-source solutions or the reliance on closed-source verification tools.
- **AI Concerns**: Approximately 20% expressed concern about AI's impact but felt its current limitations provided a buffer. Other viewpoints included a belief that LLM development has not met expectations, with some considering the current "hype" as just another major technological breakthrough (like the internet or mobile phones). They concluded that while AI will continue to evolve, humans who can keep pace will not be replaced.
- **Impact on Juniors**: There was a unanimous agreement that the current job market for junior developers is being affected. However, one perspective suggests a future "sweet spot" where a new wave of hiring for entry-level positions might occur.
- **Sponsorship Trends**: A notable observation was that most sponsors focused on introducing their solutions rather than on hiring efforts. Additionally, there was a noticeable lack of obvious "AI-integration" at the sponsor booths.
